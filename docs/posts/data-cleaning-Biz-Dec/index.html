Introduction

Banks don’t suffer from a lack of data—they suffer from a lack of trustworthy data. And trustworthy data doesn’t happen by accident. It’s the result of deliberate business decisions, disciplined technical execution, and strong governance.

In this post, we’ll explore how banks can elevate data cleaning from an ad‑hoc technical chore to a strategic capability, supported by clear processes, RACI models, and modern cloud platforms like Microsoft Azure.

Why Data Cleaning in Banks Is a Business Decision

Data cleaning is often misunderstood as a purely technical task. However ideally every cleaning action, whether merging records, imputing missing values, or standardizing formats changes the meaning of data. A lack of business‑logic application in data cleaning may render the data synthetic.

That means cleaning decisions affect:

  • Regulatory reporting
  • Risk models
  • Customer segmentation
  • Fraud detection
  • Credit decisions
  • Operational efficiency

Because of this, banks must treat data cleaning as a business-governed process with technical precision, not a back-office afterthought.

Governance: The Backbone of Trustworthy Data

A strong governance framework ensures that cleaning decisions are:

  • Intentional (aligned with business rules)
  • Consistent (repeatable across teams and systems)
  • Traceable (auditable for regulators and internal risk teams)
  • Accountable (owned by the right roles)

Traceability: The Non‑Negotiable Requirement

Banks must be able to answer:

  • Who changed the data?
  • Why was it changed?
  • What rule triggered the change?
  • What was the original value?
  • Which downstream systems were impacted?

Traceability is essential for:

  • Regulatory audits
  • Model validation
  • Internal controls
  • Operational risk management

Azure services like Azure Purview (Microsoft Purview) provide automated lineage, rule catalogs, and audit trails that make this feasible at scale

The Data Cleaning Process

  1. Profiling - Understand the data: completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, timeliness.
  2. Rule Definition - Business + data stewards define rules (e.g., “Date of birth cannot be in the future”).
  3. Rule Execution - Technical teams implement rules using Azure Data Factory, Databricks, or Synapse.
  4. Exception Handling - Flag anomalies, route to operations or business owners.
  5. Remediation - Fix data manually or automatically based on approved logic.
  6. Validation - Ensure the fix meets business and regulatory expectations.
  7. Documentation & Lineage Update - Record what changed and why.
  8. Monitoring - Continuous quality dashboards and alerts.
